Emma has a small set of features that make life easier in ways other clients don't. Having fewer of them than the competition is the point, not a phase she's growing out of. She focuses on what matters, does it well, and removes the rest.

Honesty

Emma doesn't send marketing email. She doesn't add filters to your Gmail account to promote herself and keep you from marking it as spam, the way at least one competitor does. If you start a conversation with support — me — you can unsubscribe from that thread with one tap, like anything else.

She's built by one person on infrastructure that scales without the bill scaling with it. She runs code written only as needed and saves only what's necessary.

Privacy

There's a machine at Google with your email on it, and a machine in Iowa that belongs to Emma. Your phone asks Emma, Emma asks Google, Google answers, Emma hands it to your phone and forgets. While the spinner is turning, your email is in that machine's memory. When it stops, it isn't anywhere.

Every third-party Gmail app relays through a server — Google requires it, and every one you've used works this way. The difference is what happens next. Most of them keep what passes through: Spark's privacy policy says it stores your mail on its servers, and Edison historically monetized data extracted from it. Emma keeps nothing. Storing your mail would cost me time to build and money to host. Win, win.

The privacy policy was written by a non-legal human to be read by non-legal humans. It also happens to satisfy the lawyers.
